How does the PV / solar-thermal split affect climate impact and cost-effectiveness?

The x-axis shows the share of available roof area allocated to PV (0 = all solar thermal, 1 = all PV). The green line shows annual CO₂ avoided (t/y) — higher is better. The orange dashed line shows the abatement cost (CHF per tonne CO₂ avoided), which combines annualised investment, O&M, and energy savings — lower is better. The x* badge marks the split with the lowest abatement cost; the max CO₂ badge marks the split that avoids the most emissions.

Method notes
  • Baseline CO₂ = electricity use × current grid factor + heat use × current heat-carrier factor.
  • Scenario CO₂ = residual electricity × new grid factor + residual heat × new heat-carrier factor.
  • PV self-consumption reduces residual electricity demand; excess is exported for a feed-in credit.
  • Solar thermal is capped by the declared useful heat load and degrades annually.
  • If heat pump swap is enabled, remaining heat demand becomes electric demand at the selected COP.
  • NPV discounts yearly net cashflows. Abatement cost annualizes CAPEX over the horizon.
  • PV lifecycle chart shows year-by-year kWh output; the amber dashed line marks when cumulative NPV turns positive.
  • CO₂ factors from Swiss LCA data (WWZ mix: 0.078 kgCO₂e/kWh; wood chips: 0.025 kgCO₂e/kWh). Adjustable via Expert settings.
